# Capability Showcase

A capability showcase earns a next conversation. It opens with a problem the audience recognizes, shows how Quantzig solves it, proves it with anonymized results, and offers a low-risk way to start. It sells outcomes, not a list of services.

## Two flavors

| Flavor | Opens with | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | The business problem and why it is hard | Offering decks, solution overviews, new stakeholder introductions, internal showcases |
| Point of view | A market or technology shift and what it means | Thought leadership, growth pitches, executive conversations |

The point of view flavor replaces slide 2 with "The shift" and slide 3 with "What it means for you", and keeps the rest.

## Workflow

1. Collect inputs, then research the gaps (SKILL.md, step 3). Pull from past case studies, demo assets, and solution decks.
2. Choose 2 or 3 proof points closest to the audience's industry and problem. Anonymize them.
3. Draft the storyline with conclusion titles and confirm.
4. Build, then validate.

## Inputs

| Input | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability or topic | Free text | All |
| Audience | Industry, function, seniority; client or internal | Slides 2, 3, 8 |
| Flavor | Capability / Point of view | Slides 2 and 3 |
| Problem or shift | Free text | Slides 2 and 3 |
| Offering components | Services, accelerators, platforms | Slides 4 and 5 |
| Demo assets | Live screenshots / Illustrative / None | Slide 7 |
| Proof points | Case studies with numbers | Slide 8 |
| Engagement options | Workshop, PoC, pilot, managed service | Slide 10 |

## Storyline (cap 10, tolerance 2)

| # | Slide | Audience question | Hero visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover | What is this about? | Cover with an outcome or insight title |
| 2 | Why this matters now | Why should I care? | Big-number facts about the problem or shift, with sources |
| 3 | What usually goes wrong | Do you understand my world? | Pitfall cards, 3 common failure points |
| 4 | What we offer | What can you do? | Capability map of 3 to 6 pillars |
| 5 | How we do it | How does it work? | Chevron journey with accelerators tagged at each step |
| 6 | Under the hood | Is it robust? | Layered architecture or a worked example |
| 7 | See it in action | What does it look like? | Framed screenshots with callout pins |
| 8 | Proof | Have you done this before? | 2 or 3 proof cards with anonymized descriptors and big numbers |
| 9 | Where it applies | Is it relevant to us? | Use case grid by industry or function, with the audience's row highlighted |
| 10 | Ways to start | What is the first step? | Engagement ladder (workshop, PoC, pilot, scale) with duration per step, plus a next-steps strip |
| A | Appendix | Detail | Detailed case studies, technology partners, team credentials, method detail |

### Slide notes

- **Why this matters now**: 2 or 3 facts with sources (market data, benchmarks, Quantzig experience across clients). No unsourced statistics.
- **What we offer**: pillars are outcomes the audience buys ("faster root cause analysis"), each with the accelerators or assets behind it.
- **See it in action**: prefer live demo builds. Mark mockups "Illustrative". No client data or branding visible.
- **Proof**: use the Proof card block. Descriptors follow the anonymization hint guide.
- **Ways to start**: the first step is small and time-boxed. This is the ask.

## Flex rules

- Short introduction (15-minute slot): use slides 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, and 10 only.
- Internal showcase (for Quantzig leadership or sales teams): add a slide after 9 on target accounts and pipeline, and a talk track in the speaker notes for sellers.
- No demo assets: drop slide 7 and expand slide 6 into a worked example.
- Point of view flavor: slide 3 becomes "What leading companies are doing about it" with 3 moves, and the title of slide 4 connects the moves to the offering.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The deck opens with the audience's problem or a shift, not with Quantzig's history.
- [ ] Every fact on slide 2 has a source.
- [ ] Every proof point is anonymized with a descriptive hint and has a number with basis.
- [ ] Screens contain no client data or branding. Mockups are tagged "Illustrative".
- [ ] The deck ends with a small, time-boxed first step and next steps.
