# Case Study

A case study proves one thing: we solved a problem like yours and can show the number. The story runs result first, then challenge, then what we did, then how it works, then what changed. Buyers skim, so the headline number must be on the first content slide, not the last.

## Sizes

| Size | Slides | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof card | 1 | Inside proposals, PoCs, growth pitches, capability decks. Use the Proof card block in `references/blocks.md` |
| Summary | 2 | Proof card plus one "how it works" slide, for pitch decks that need a little more depth |
| Full story | 6 (tolerance 2) | Standalone sharing, sales collateral, RFP responses, website source |

Default to the full story when the user asks for "a case study" with no context. Default to the proof card when it goes inside another deck.

## Workflow

1. Collect inputs (below), then research the gaps (SKILL.md, step 3). Pull from attachments first: past decks, SOWs, results trackers.
2. Build the anonymized descriptor using the hint guide in `references/blocks.md`, section 12.
3. Pick the headline number: the single result a buyer in this industry cares about most.
4. Draft the storyline with conclusion titles and confirm with the user.
5. Build, then validate with the shared checklist in SKILL.md plus the one below.

## Inputs

| Input | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client descriptor | Region, size, industry, what they do | Cover, every slide |
| Business problem | Free text | Slide 3 |
| Why it mattered | Cost, revenue, risk, speed, compliance | Slide 3 |
| What we built | Model / Dashboard / Platform / Gen AI / Optimization / Mix | Slides 4 and 5 |
| Results with basis | Numbers, and whether measured, client-reported, or estimated | Slides 2 and 6 |
| Timeline and team | Weeks, roles | Slide 4 footer |
| Visual assets | Screenshots, demo link, architecture | Slide 5 |
| Named or anonymized | Anonymized (default) / Named with written approval | All |
| Target audience | Industry peer / Different industry / Internal | Relevance lines |

Results are the one thing research can never fill: they must come from the actual engagement. If a result has no basis, ask. If none exists, write the result as an observed outcome without a number rather than inventing one.

## Full story storyline

| # | Slide | Reader question | Hero visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover | Who was this for, and what happened? | Cover with outcome title and anonymized descriptor |
| 2 | Result at a glance | Is this worth my time? | One large headline number plus 2 or 3 KPI tiles and a one-line context |
| 3 | The challenge | Is their problem like mine? | Split panel: what was happening today and what it cost, versus what they needed |
| 4 | What we did | How did you approach it? | Chevron journey of 3 to 5 steps, with duration and team strip |
| 5 | How it works | Is this real and repeatable? | Layered architecture, or a worked example showing input, engine, and output, or framed screenshots |
| 6 | What changed | Did it stick? | Before-after bars plus process change (manual to automated) and an optional role-attributed quote |
| 7 | Optional: why it matters for you | Can you do it for us? | Three relevance cards plus a next step, only when the case study is sent to a specific prospect |

### Slide notes

- **Cover**: title states the outcome ("Container Optimization Lifted Truck Utilization by 7% for a Global Brewer"). Date and descriptor underneath.
- **Result at a glance**: the headline number at 54 to 60 pt, its label, and its basis. Two or three supporting tiles across financial, operational, and speed or effort. One sentence: who, what problem, what we built.
- **The challenge**: 3 to 4 rows of pain on the left (manual steps, delays, blind spots, cost) and matching needs on the right. One line on the business stakes.
- **What we did**: each chevron has a step name, an icon, and 2 lines. Footer strip: duration, team roles, technology tags.
- **How it works**: pick the form that makes it most tangible. Worked example beats architecture for business audiences. Architecture beats worked example for technical audiences. Mark mockups "Illustrative".
- **What changed**: numbers from slide 2 reappear here with before and after. Add adoption (users, frequency, decisions now made with it). Quotes are attributed by role only.

## Flex rules

- Only one result number available: slide 2 shows that number plus qualitative outcome tiles (for example "weekly planning moved from 2 days to 2 hours" is still a number, so look for time and effort savings).
- Work still in progress: title slide 6 "Early results" and state the measurement period.
- Many modules (like a multi-module analytics program): slide 5 becomes a capability map of the modules, with detail in an appendix.
- Summary size: slide 1 is the proof card, slide 2 is "How it works" from the full story.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The headline number is on the first content slide and repeated with its before value on the last.
- [ ] Every number has a basis. No invented or rounded-up results.
- [ ] Client is anonymized with a descriptive hint unless written approval exists. No logos, product names, people, or branded screenshots.
- [ ] The challenge is written so a peer in the same industry recognizes it.
- [ ] The approach names duration and team, so the reader can size a similar effort.
- [ ] Proof card version fits one slide and can be dropped into another deck without edits.
