## Slide 1: Cover

- **Content**: title stated as an outcome ("Weekly Demand Signals for Faster Planning"), client name, date, Quantzig logo from the template master.
- **Visual**: gradient cover with one line illustration that hints at the domain (supply chain flow, molecule, shelf, pipeline). Build it from native shapes.

## Slide 2: Executive summary

Write this slide last. A senior stakeholder should be able to forward it alone.

- **Problem**: one sentence in the client's words.
- **Approach**: one sentence naming the stages.
- **Impact**: 2 or 3 quantified outcomes, shown as big numbers.
- **Ask**: phase 1 scope, duration and investment range, or the discovery workshop.
- **Visual**: four-tile story strip read left to right (Problem, Approach, Impact, Ask), each with an icon. The Ask tile is highlighted with the gradient.

## Slide 3: Where you are, where you want to be

- **Objective**: two lines at most, stated as a business outcome. "Improve promotion ROI by deciding spend on reliable weekly data" beats "Build a promotion dashboard".
- **Rows**: 3 to 5 pairs. Each current state fact (systems, manual steps, turnaround, known pain) pairs with its future state.
- **Visual**: split panel. The left (today) is muted, with a pain icon per row. The right (tomorrow) is in brand color, with an outcome icon per row. A bridge arrow runs between the panels. The objective sits in a banner above.
- Every future state row must reappear on slide 9 with a number. Tag each row (F1, F2, ...) to keep the link traceable.

## Slide 4: What usually goes wrong

- **Pitfalls**: the top 3 that similar organizations hit. For each, give what they do, why it fails (one line), and how we avoid it (one line).
- **Visual**: three equal cards, each with a warning icon, the pitfall in bold, the consequence, and an "our fix" tag at the base.
- **Proof strip**: 2 or 3 result chips along the bottom. Each chip has an industry icon, one big number and a short label ("18% fewer stock-outs, global CPG"), and points to an appendix case study.

## Slide 5: How we get you there

Define the stage spine here. Later slides reuse its names, numbers, icons and accent colors.

Default five-stage spine:

| Stage | What we do | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Data foundation | Assess sources, fix quality and definitions, build governed pipelines | One trusted version of the numbers |
| 2. Intelligence engine | Models, rules, KPIs or AI agents that answer the business question | Insight the business did not have before |
| 3. Solution experience | Dashboard, application, copilot or embedded workflow | Insight reaches the people who decide |
| 4. Deploy and operate | Integration, automated refresh, model and data monitoring | Runs reliably without manual effort |
| 5. Adopt and prove value | Training, decision rhythms, value tracking | People use it, and value is measured |

Adapt the spine to the problem. A pure BI engagement may merge stages 2 and 3. Gen AI work may add an evaluation and guardrails stage. Never go above 5.

- **Visual**: chevron journey left to right. Each chevron carries a number badge, an icon, the stage name and 2 lines of what we do. Above each chevron, one outcome line links to a slide 3 future state tag.

## Slide 6: How it fits together

- **Left**: client data sources, named by system (ERP, CRM, syndicated data, files).
- **Middle**: ingestion, storage, modelling and serving layers, with named technologies. Use the client's stack if known. Otherwise show the recommended stack and note that it adapts.
- **Right**: consumers by role (planners, brand managers, field reps) or downstream systems.
- **Bottom band**: security, access governance, monitoring, and the adoption loop.
- **Visual**: layered line diagram. Each stage is a dotted-boundary module with its number badge. Flow arrows run left to right. About 15 boxes at most.

## Slides 7 and 8: Inside the solution

Split stages across two slides (for example, 1 and 2, then 3 to 5). With 3 stages or fewer, use one slide.

- **Visual**: stage tracker along the top, with the current stages highlighted. One column card per stage flows from an input icon, to the work, to an output icon.
- **Card fields**:
  - Objective: one line.
  - Approach: the method, model or design choice, and why.
  - Output: the tangible artifact (data model, trained model, working app, runbook).
  - Done when: an exit criterion the client can check ("data reconciles to finance within 1%").
  - We need from you: a small tag, only where it applies (data access, SME hours, approvals).
- Detailed activity plans and tool lists go to the appendix.

## Slide 9: What you get

This slide closes the loop with slide 3.

- **Product or platform**: 2 or 3 real screenshots in a browser or device frame, with numbered callout pins naming the decision each screen supports. Prefer a working demo build over mockups.
- **Technical or analytical solution**: 3 or 4 big-number KPI tiles, one per future state tag, across financial, quality, effort and speed.
- **Each KPI tile shows**:
  - a from-to value or range ("MAPE 35% to 22-25%"),
  - the future state tag it answers,
  - the basis in one line (past engagement, benchmark, pilot, or client baseline).
- **Number rules**:
  - Always state the basis.
  - Use ranges when there is no measured baseline.
  - If today's value is unknown, write "baselined in discovery". Never invent it.
  - Put the estimate logic in the appendix.

## Slide 10: Roadmap and deliverables

| Phase | Purpose | Typical duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crawl | Prove value on a narrow scope | 6 to 10 weeks |
| Walk | Scale what worked, automate, integrate | 10 to 16 weeks |
| Run | Full rollout, handover or managed service | 12+ weeks |
| Support (optional) | Monitor, fix, refresh, enhance | Ongoing |

- **Visual**: swimlane timeline. The stages from slide 5 run as lanes, with their badges. Phase bands run across the top. Gates are diamonds, each labelled with the client decision needed to proceed.
- **Deliverables**: under each phase band, 3 or 4 deliverable icons with short names. Name the thing that will exist, never an activity.
- **Client asks**: at each gate, a small tag stating what we need to pass it.
- **Small engagement**: one plan with fortnightly milestones.
- **Scope unclear**:
  - First block: a 2 to 3 week discovery workshop. Show its outputs as icons: current state assessment, prioritized use case backlog, defined Crawl scope with success criteria, and a firm phase 1 plan.
  - Then a dotted Crawl block with an indicative range (for example, 8 to 10 weeks).

## Slide 11: Investment and team

- **Investment per phase**: a firm figure, a range, or "TBD after discovery". Include the commercial model per phase (fixed price, T&M, capacity-based, managed service).
- **Visual**: stepped cost ladder, one step per phase rising left to right, each showing the phase, duration and investment. TBD steps are dotted. A compact table is acceptable if procurement expects one.
- **Team**: a row of skill profile icons with labels (engagement lead, data engineering, data science, BI and UX, Gen AI engineering, domain SME, change and adoption). No headcounts or rates.
- **Assumptions strip** at the base: the 3 or 4 assumptions the price depends on, plus validity, inclusions and exclusions, at 10.5 pt.

## Slide 12: Why Quantzig and next steps

End on value and action, not price.

- **Left: 4 to 6 value pillar cards**, each specific to this proposal:
  - cross-industry learning,
  - similar problems solved (linked to the appendix),
  - domain and technology SMEs,
  - accelerators and reusable assets,
  - thought leadership and benchmarks,
  - delivery discipline.
- **Right**: a vertical timeline of 3 to 5 dated next steps with owners, ending in a decision (for example, a phase 1 go or no-go).
- **Footer**: one full-width decision band. No contact cards unless real names and details are supplied. Close the deck with the Reach Out layout.

## Appendix

**Case studies**: 2 or 3, one slide each, chosen for closeness to the client's problem.

- **Visual**: three-step story band (Challenge, then What we did, then Result) with an industry icon. The result shows 2 or 3 big numbers. A mini stage-spine strip highlights which stages the work covered.
- **Content**:
  - context in two lines,
  - the analytical solution and how it was implemented,
  - the quantified result,
  - one line on relevance to this client.
- **Naming**: anonymize by default. Name a client only with written approval.

**Other slides, as needed**: the full client asks list by phase, the risk register (risk, mitigation, owner), detailed architecture and tool choices, activity plans per stage, value estimate logic, and team bios.
