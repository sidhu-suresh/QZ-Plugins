# Roadmap

A roadmap deck gets agreement on direction and sequence: where we are, where we are going, why this order, what it takes, and what we decide today. The hero is always one roadmap visual. Every other slide either justifies it or explains what it needs.

## Pick the roadmap type

| Type | Audience | What the roadmap items are | What changes in the storyline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Analytics roadmap | Client function or CDO | Use cases, data foundations, maturity steps | Slide 3 is a maturity ladder; slide 5 prioritizes a use case backlog |
| Product or platform roadmap | Client product owner and sponsors | Releases and features | Slide 7 is a feature scope grid by release; include architecture evolution in slide 9 |
| Account growth roadmap | Quantzig leadership or account team (internal) | Accounts, divisions, stakeholders, offerings | Slide 3 is a whitespace map; slide 8 shows pipeline and revenue or FTE targets |
| Capability roadmap | Quantzig leadership (internal) or client (for a CoE) | Skills, assets, accelerators, offerings, hiring | Slide 3 is a capability maturity view; slide 9 covers hiring and investment |

Ask which type applies if it is not clear. Internal roadmaps (account growth, capability) may show revenue, FTE, and hiring numbers. Client roadmaps never show rates or margins.

## Workflow

1. Collect inputs, then research the gaps (SKILL.md, step 3). Pull from existing plans, backlogs, previous roadmaps, and account trackers.
2. Build the prioritization logic before the roadmap. Items without a reason for their position are the first thing a sponsor questions.
3. Draft the storyline with conclusion titles and confirm.
4. Build, then validate.

## Inputs

| Input | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roadmap type | Analytics / Product or platform / Account growth / Capability | Slides 3, 5, 7, 8, 9 |
| Horizon | 6 months / 12 months / 18 to 24 months | Slide 6 |
| Current state | Maturity, existing assets, known gaps | Slide 3 |
| Vision or target state | Free text with outcomes | Slide 4 |
| Candidate items | Backlog, features, accounts, capabilities | Slides 5 to 7 |
| Prioritization criteria | Value, effort, dependency, risk, sponsor priority | Slide 5 |
| Value per horizon | Numbers with basis, or to be estimated | Slide 8 |
| What it takes | Team, investment, dependencies | Slide 9 |

## Storyline (cap 10, tolerance 2)

| # | Slide | Audience question | Hero visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover | What roadmap is this? | Cover with the destination as title |
| 2 | Executive summary | Where are we going, and what do you need? | Direction strip: where we are, where we are going, what it takes, the ask |
| 3 | Where we are today | What is our starting point? | Maturity ladder with "you are here", or a split panel, or a whitespace map (account growth) |
| 4 | Where we are going | What does good look like? | Vision banner plus 3 or 4 outcome tiles |
| 5 | How we prioritized | Why this order? | Value versus effort 2x2 with numbered items, plus the criteria used |
| 6 | The roadmap | What happens when? | Swimlane or release train across the horizon, or Now, Next, Later columns |
| 7 | What each horizon delivers | What do we get at each step? | Scope grid: horizon or release by feature, use case, or capability |
| 8 | Value and milestones | What does each step unlock? | KPI tiles per horizon with basis, milestones as diamonds |
| 9 | What it takes | What do we need to make it happen? | Team, investment range, dependencies, and risks in four columns |
| 10 | Decisions and next steps | What do we decide today? | Next-steps timeline ending in the decision needed |
| A | Appendix | Detail | Full backlog with scores, feature detail, architecture evolution, assumptions |

### Slide notes

- **Where we are today**: honest and specific. For a product roadmap, show what is live today and its adoption.
- **How we prioritized**: name the criteria and show the scoring in the appendix. Quick wins go first to build momentum and fund later horizons.
- **The roadmap**: at most 5 lanes. Lanes are workstreams, product areas, account groups, or capability pillars depending on type. Later horizons show less detail and lighter styling to signal lower certainty.
- **What each horizon delivers**: name things that will exist, not activities. Items still to be scoped carry a "to be scoped" tag.
- **Value and milestones**: each horizon unlocks something the sponsor cares about. Use ranges where estimates are early.
- **What it takes**: dependencies on the client or on Quantzig leadership are explicit, with owners.

## Flex rules

- Short horizon (6 months or less): merge slides 6 and 7.
- Roadmap already agreed, this is an update: slide 3 becomes "Progress since last review" with done, moved, and new items, and slide 5 explains any re-sequencing.
- Product roadmap with AI features phased in later releases: show AI features in their own lane so the sponsor sees when they arrive.
- Account growth roadmap across several accounts: slide 7 becomes one row per account with the target offerings and stakeholders per horizon.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The roadmap type is clear and the storyline uses the matching visuals for slides 3, 5, 7, 8, and 9.
- [ ] Every roadmap item has a reason for its position (criteria on slide 5).
- [ ] Every horizon has named deliverables and a value or milestone.
- [ ] Later horizons are visibly less certain than near-term ones.
- [ ] Dependencies and asks have owners.
- [ ] Internal numbers (revenue, FTE, hiring) appear only in internal roadmaps.
