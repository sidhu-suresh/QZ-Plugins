# Proposal

Quantzig proposals tell one story in 12 slides or fewer: we understand your problem, we have seen it before, here is how we will solve it, and here is what it costs and returns. This file decides the story and the visual form of each slide. the formatting guide (`formatting/GUIDE.md`) decides brand formatting. pptx decides how the file is built.

Why the visual bar matters: a proposal made of tables and text boxes reads like a report. Clients remember a proposal by its pictures: the journey, the architecture, the before and after. Every slide needs one hero visual that carries the message on its own.

## Workflow

1. **Collect inputs, then research the gaps (SKILL.md, step 3).** Ask only for what is missing from the conversation and attachments. Use tappable options where the tool exists. See "Inputs" below.
2. **Build the stage spine.** Define 3 to 5 solution stages with names, numbers, icons and one outcome each. Everything later reuses this spine.
3. **Draft the storyline.** Write all 12 slide titles as conclusions (full sentences, 12 words or fewer), plus a one-line note on each hero visual. Show it to the user and confirm before building. This is the cheapest point to change direction.
4. **Build slides.** Follow `references/types/proposal-slide-specs.md` for content and `references/visual-recipes.md` for how to draw each visual with native shapes.
5. **Validate.** Run the checklist at the end of this file. Fix failures, then report anything that still needs the user (missing numbers, unapproved client names, TBD commercials).

## Inputs

| Input | Options | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client problem and objective | Free text | Slides 2, 3 |
| Current state and desired future state | 3 to 5 each | Slides 3 and 9 |
| Solution type | Product or platform / Technical or analytical / Gen AI / BI / Mix | Slide 9 format, stage spine |
| Scope clarity | Clear / Partly clear / Unclear | Unclear means the discovery workshop path on slide 10 and TBD pricing |
| Engagement size | Small (under 12 weeks) / Large | Single plan or Crawl, Walk, Run on slide 10 |
| Support phase needed | Yes / No | Slides 10 and 11 |
| Case studies available | Uploaded / From library / None yet | Proof strip on slide 4, appendix |
| Commercial status | Firm / Range / TBD | Slide 11 |
| Screenshots available | Yes / No | Slide 9 |

If a number is not available, write a clearly marked placeholder such as `[baseline: to confirm in discovery]`. Never invent client figures.

## The 12-slide storyline

| # | Slide | Client question | Hero visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover | Who is this for? | Gradient cover with one domain line illustration |
| 2 | Executive summary | What are you proposing, and why care? | Four-tile story strip: Problem, Approach, Impact, Ask |
| 3 | Where you are, where you want to be | Do you understand us? | Today vs tomorrow split panel with icon rows and a bridge arrow |
| 4 | What usually goes wrong | Have you seen this before? | Three pitfall cards plus a proof strip of result chips |
| 5 | How we get you there | How, step by step? | Chevron journey of 3 to 5 stages |
| 6 | How it fits together | How do the pieces connect? | Layered line architecture with stages as modules |
| 7 | Inside the solution (1 of 2) | What happens in each stage? | Stage tracker plus input, work, output column cards |
| 8 | Inside the solution (2 of 2) | The remaining stages | Same layout as slide 7 |
| 9 | What you get | What is it worth? | Framed screenshots with callout pins, or big-number KPI tiles |
| 10 | Roadmap and deliverables | How long, and what will we hold? | Swimlane timeline with phase gates and deliverable icons |
| 11 | Investment and team | Cost, and who does it? | Stepped cost ladder, skill profile icon row, assumptions strip |
| 12 | Why Quantzig and next steps | Why you, and what happens Monday? | Value pillar cards, next-step timeline, decision band |
| A | Appendix | Can you prove it? | Case study story bands and supporting detail |

### Flex rules

- Twelve is a hard cap, including the cover. Never add a slide to fit content. Cut, merge, or move it to the appendix.
- Three stages or fewer: merge slides 7 and 8. Use the freed slide for "What we need from you and key risks".
- Small engagement: slide 10 shows one plan with fortnightly milestones.
- Scope unclear: slide 10 shows a 2 to 3 week discovery workshop, then a dotted, indicative Crawl block.
- Client asks live on the deep-dive cards and the roadmap gates. Key assumptions live on slide 11. The full asks list and the risk register go to the appendix.

## Visual standard

The test for every slide: hide the body text. The visual alone should still carry the point.

| Message | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before vs after | Split panel, icon per row, bridge arrow | Two bullet lists |
| Steps or stages | Chevrons or numbered journey with icons | Numbered text list |
| How parts connect | Layered line diagram with grouped modules | Table of components |
| A key number | Big-number callout (40 to 60 pt) with a one-line label | Number inside a table cell |
| Options or pillars | Equal icon cards | Bullets under headings |
| Time and phases | Swimlane timeline with milestone diamonds | Table of dates |
| Proof | Result chips: industry icon, big number, short label | Paragraph of case text |
| A product | Screenshot in a browser or device frame with numbered pins | Bare screenshot |
| Money by phase | Stepped bars or cost ladder | Table alone |

Rules, with the reason for each:

- **One hero visual per slide**, where the eye lands first. Two competing visuals split attention.
- **Tables on at most two main slides** (usually 9 and 11). Every extra table pushes the deck back toward a report.
- **Enough copy to read without a presenter:** 90 to 160 words per content slide (up to 220 on deep dives), never under 70, spread across the visual's labels and the supporting panels. See Shared rules in the main SKILL.md.
- **One line-icon set, one style.** Each stage keeps the same icon, number badge and accent color from slide 5 to slide 10, so the client tracks stages without reading.
- **Stage colors only on icons, badges and thin accents, and only brand colors** (navy, wine, purple, note purple). Fills stay within the formatting guide (`formatting/GUIDE.md`) limits (white, gradient, or theme color at 96% transparency).
- **Native shapes and connectors only**, never pasted pictures of diagrams, so the client team can edit them.
- **No stock photos, clipart, 3D charts or decorative images** that carry no information.

## Writing rules

- Titles are conclusions: "Three gaps slow most forecasting programs", not "Key gaps".
- Use the client's terms. Quantify wherever possible and state the basis for every number.
- Plain, human sentences. Use full words instead of contractions. No em dashes, middle dots, arrows, filler or buzzword stacks.
- Anonymize clients in proof points ("a global biopharma company") unless the user confirms written approval.

## Validation checklist

Run this before handing over. Report each failure and how it was fixed, or what the user must supply.

- [ ] 12 slides or fewer, excluding the appendix.
- [ ] Every slide has one hero visual that carries the point without the body text.
- [ ] Tables appear on two main slides at most.
- [ ] Executive summary stands alone and states the ask.
- [ ] Every future state on slide 3 maps to a number on slide 9.
- [ ] Stage names, numbers, icons and colors match from slide 5 through slide 10.
- [ ] Each stage card has an objective, approach, output and done-when criterion.
- [ ] Every phase has named deliverables and an investment line (figure, range or TBD).
- [ ] Why Quantzig pillars are specific to this problem and link to appendix proof.
- [ ] Next steps have owners and dates.
- [ ] No client names without approval. No invented figures. No em dashes, middle dots, or arrows. No placeholder contacts.
- [ ] Formatting follows `formatting/GUIDE.md`. Render and visually inspect every slide for overlaps and overflow.
